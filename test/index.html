1. Understanding Uncertainty

Before building robust models, we must distinguish between "bad data" and "model confusion." This section explores the taxonomy established by Hüllermeier and Waegeman, which is the foundation of this research. Click the cards below to dive into the two types of uncertainty.

A

Aleatoric Uncertainty

+

Inherent noise in the data (e.g., blurry images, garbage labels).

Nature: Irreducible. More data won't fix this.

Solution in RUA-BERT: Detect high variance ($\sigma^2$) and down-weight these samples in the loss function.

Example: An image labeled "Dog" that is actually just static noise.

E

Epistemic Uncertainty

+

The model's lack of knowledge due to limited training data.

Nature: Reducible. improved by seeing more diverse examples.

Solution in RUA-BERT: Measured via Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout to estimate confidence/calibration.

Example: The model has never seen a "Platypus" before and is unsure how to classify it.

2. The "Memorization" Trap

Transformers like BERT are powerful but "arrogant." When training on noisy data (e.g., 30% wrong labels), they initially learn simple patterns (clean data) but eventually start memorizing the noise to minimize loss.

The chart illustrates this phenomenon described by Zhu et al. [4]. Standard BERT's validation accuracy drops as it overfits to noise. RUA-BERT aims to maintain performance by ignoring that noise.

Critical Gap

"Standard Cross-Entropy loss treats every training sample as absolute truth, forcing the model to memorize errors."

5. Implementation Plan

The research targets the Quora Question Pairs (QQP) dataset, simulating a low-resource environment with significant data corruption.

Experimental Setup

Dataset: QQP (Quora Question Pairs)

Condition: 30% Corrupted Labels

Goal: Prove robustness without clean validation sets.
